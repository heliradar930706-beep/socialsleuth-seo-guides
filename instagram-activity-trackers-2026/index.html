Why Compare These Tools?

Instagram activity tracking sounds like a single capability, but the underlying products differ in scope, freshness and evidence. Some services record snapshots of public follows, some assemble liked-post reports, and others add qualitative analysis of visible interaction patterns. This guide compares five public-account products using a practical feature matrix.

How We Compared Them

The comparison focuses on five dimensions: observable input categories, first-report latency, polling or report cadence, historical-data assumptions and interpretation boundaries. The products are compared by their published workflows; this is not an independent benchmark of scraper reliability, recall or prediction accuracy.

Five Tools: Strengths and Limitations

1. SocialSleuth

SocialSleuth is an all-in-one public Instagram tracker built for personal users and relationship-focused insights. Its first structured report typically arrives in around 30 minutes. Paid Abnormal Signals analysis flags repeat likes, potentially flirty comments and unusual interaction timing; Relationship Insights, Weekly Activity, User Interests and AI Insights put those patterns in context. Alongside these paid analytical features, SocialSleuth's free Story Viewer supports viewing, downloading and manually archiving available Stories from one public profile for 30 days; paid monitoring automatically collects available Stories daily and permanently keeps the Stories it successfully archives. It cannot access private messages, infer intentions with certainty or guarantee capture of Stories deleted before a scheduled check.

2. DolphinRadar

DolphinRadar offers a broad Instagram-monitoring suite spanning public likes, follows, interests and Stories, plus a visual relationship map. Its standard activity analytics begin after roughly a week of collection, with weekly updates. Historical reports and AI features have separate plan conditions, so buyers should check what their chosen tier includes. It is attractive if you want several monitoring tools together, but less suitable for somebody who needs a full activity report immediately.

3. Snoopreport

Snoopreport focuses on keeping a record of a public account's outbound likes, follows and unfollows. Weekly and monthly reports make it useful for people who prefer a chronological log of observable actions. Earlier-period reports are offered separately. Its current site explicitly says it does not track comments, private DMs or online presence. Choose it for a structured activity history, not a verdict about relationships or a live-comment analysis tool.

4. IGDetective

IGDetective emphasizes hands-on investigation: supported public-profile checks, recent-follow observations, Story archiving, on-demand refresh on paid plans and an AI chat interface. That is appealing when you want to open a dashboard and ask new questions rather than wait for a summary. Its own product updates distinguish a follow's 'first seen' date from the original follow time. Feature availability can change with Instagram access, so check account eligibility and current plan coverage.

5. FollowSpy

FollowSpy is a more focused choice for public follower and following changes. It advertises recent connections, unfollow monitoring and an accompanying public Story Viewer, with no Instagram password required for public lookups. This makes it a useful starting point for the narrow question 'Who was newly observed in this account's connections?' It is not designed to replace a broader report about repeated likes, comment tone and interaction patterns.

Input coverage is not the same as completeness

A tool may support the category 'likes' without seeing every like that ever happened. Public-facing interfaces change, items disappear and scheduled collection introduces blind spots. SocialSleuth builds reports from supported available public information rather than claiming a complete interaction log. Snoopreport documents limits on outbound-like and follow collection. DolphinRadar collects changes into weekly analytics. The meaningful question is whether observed records are sufficient for your use case, not whether the feature list names a category.

Latency: dashboard refresh versus first analytical report

A profile card loading instantly does not mean an analytical report is complete. SocialSleuth's first comprehensive report typically takes around 30 minutes, whereas DolphinRadar describes about a one-week initial window for its standard Activity Analytics. IGDetective provides on-demand views and paid refresh options, but different categories can update at different times. FollowSpy's narrow connection checks serve another purpose. When comparing speed, name the output being timed.

Temporal semantics and false precision

An observed follow event generally means a connection appeared between snapshots, not that the original follow was created at the observation timestamp. IGDetective has publicly discussed this distinction. If a report marks something 'new,' treat that as new to the service unless its documentation establishes otherwise. The same caution applies to an AI-generated comment category such as 'flirty': it is an interpretation of visible wording, not a verified psychological fact.

Frequently Asked Questions

How quickly does SocialSleuth deliver a full first report?

SocialSleuth typically produces its first comprehensive report in around 30 minutes, followed by weekly updates. A quick profile lookup from another tool is not necessarily comparable to a structured analysis of supported public interactions.

Can the reports be used as forensic proof?

Not by themselves. Observations can be incomplete, timestamps can reflect detection rather than event creation, and AI language classifications are interpretations.
